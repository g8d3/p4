Where does one turn actually go?

Drop the turn log your harness already writes — the file the pi-web-tps-monitor plugin appends to, <workspace>/.pi-web/tps-turns.jsonl (one JSON object per line) — and this page computes the per-turn decomposition and the tail, on your numbers. Other harness? Drop a CSV (header row) and you map your own column names on the next screen; nothing is guessed from a column's position.

It is the same arithmetic the panel and the audit use: rest = turn wall − stream − tools − pre-response, and a turn is wasted when it failed, aborted, or produced zero output tokens (the rule in models.ts).

4. What this file cannot tell you

    Where the token enters matters too

    The same model costs different amounts on different routes, and the cheapest route is not always the fastest one. We measured it on the providers we hold keys for — input price runs 29.1x between routes for one model, and for another the first token is 6.0x slower on the cheap route than on the fast one. Table, caveats and the raw rows: https://focalis.cc/audit/routes/. If your rows carry the provider field (our monitor writes it per turn since v0.10.8), section 2 above splits your own wall by the route that served it; a log that predates the field does not, and the route is one of the things the audit asks you for.

    What the audit adds to this preview

    This preview is the part you can run yourself, today. The audit measures the part the file does not contain: the process boot before the first event (372 ms median in our runs with the startup cache warm, 940 ms cold, which never appears in a turn row) and a before/after series on your own loops, ranked by measured effect — plus the regression gate that keeps the cut from creeping back. Where we tried something and it did not reproduce (cache discipline, startup-scan scoping, a size coefficient) we say so and do not sell it: see sections 6-8 of the paper.
