> I'd like us to try, in a new experiment, making a video from HTML, but the HTML needs to be as beautiful as possible. The options I've thought of are presentations — or, I don't know, presentations or something like impress.js, or three.js. And I'd like whatever you create to be a self-explanation — honestly, whatever you do is going to be an explanation of itself, i.e., a presentation of how you make the presentation. But you have to orient it to a non-technical audience.
>

But we need the right templates so that the presentation and the video come out pretty, because — as I've said before, to other agents — the presentation or the aesthetics aren't the best.

> I saw that yesterday I was trying Open Design, but I'm not sure that's what you should do, because if you go down that path, honestly, Open Design is a very heavy program. So maybe you could search the web for templates — that you search the web for templates. That could also be put in the video or the presentation you're going to make.

## Confirmed brief (from the intent interview)

- Deliverable: one MP4 video rendered from HTML (HyperFrames), 16:9, Spanish narration, ~60-90s.
- Audience: non-technical Spanish-speaking people who own an Android phone.
- Message: "With just an Android phone and a computer connected via SSH, you can have an AI programmer or designer working for you, almost for free."
- Angle: how-to / inspiration — "you can do this too".
- The video should leave blank placeholder spaces for the user's own photos/videos of their real workflow (Termux, phone, SSH, dictation).
- Aesthetics matter most: the user wants the HTML to be as beautiful as possible; explicitly NOT relying on Open Design (too heavy); web-searched templates/inspiration are welcome and can be a beat inside the video itself.
