2. The strategy on a real chart — one example trade

Rules in practice (BTC, EMA7, 1d, 1x sizing): the signal is EMA7 crossing the weekly VWAP at the daily close — you enter the entire account (1x, no margin) at that close. The exit is only the trailing stop (T = ATR×0.02): it activates when price first moves T in your favor ("armed"), then follows the best price at distance T (dashed line below). No fixed take profit, no stop loss; if the opposite signal fires before arming, the position closes at that day's close (reversal exit).

3. What is Monte Carlo? (and what we actually did)

How the shuffle works, exactly

We take the REAL trades the strategy actually made (e.g. the 108 BTC trades of the EMA7 1d run) and write down each one's return (a list of ~108 small percentages). Then:

trades = [your 108 realized returns]          # real trades, nothing synthetic
repeat 10,000 times:
    order  = random_shuffle(trades)           # a new random ORDER of the same trades
    equity = 10,000
    for r in order:
        equity *= (1 + r)                     # compounding, 1x sizing
    record (final equity, max drawdown) of this path
p(negative) = fraction of the 10,000 paths that end below 10,000

Why: the real world played ONE of 108! possible orderings. Reshuffling asks "is my result dependent on the lucky order?" If almost every shuffle loses, the per-trade edge is absent — which is exactly what the corrected data shows below.

With corrected fills the honest answer is: edge ≈ 0. BTC EMA7: p(negative) ; median final equity (from $10k). The earlier "p(negative)=0.0" belonged to the contaminated (look-ahead) trade list — another layer of the same lesson.

Separately (falsification battery): a PERMUTATION test with RANDOM signals showed random timing + the same trailing exit already earns ~80% of the bar-level profit — i.e. the signal adds ~22%, the trailing exit does most of the work.

4. The synthetic-data negative control (Phase 8)

The missing null: 60 pure-noise price series (random walk with BTC's real daily volatility, 2.47%/day) run through the same EMA7 daily machinery. If the machine prints profits on garbage, most of the edge is the machine, not the signal.

Result: on pure noise the machine still shows PF 1.42 median (90% of noise series profitable) — it is a drift-harvester. The real market structure is what lifts it to PF 14.4 (real BTC). The full chain: noise (PF 1.4) < random entries on real data (PF 9.9) < real signal (PF 14.4).

4b. The two bugs we found in our own test (and fixed)

When you asked for the strategy example, the demo trade revealed it: our intraday walker was (1) walking the same 5-minute bars that happened BEFORE the entry (entry at day close, but the stop could trade on that morning's bars — a look-ahead that invented trades), and (2) filling same-bar stop hits at the bar OPEN instead of the stop price. Both fixed in phase5_daily_weekly.py and intrabar.py; every intrabar number above is the corrected one. The lesson is the one your instinct kept pushing: every backtest layer must be tested against itself — including ours.

7. Glossary — the robustness test family

TestKills this lie
Permutation / null signals"the signal is smart" — random entries still earn 0.65%/day vs real 0.80% (p=0.0) → signal = +22%
Monte Carlo bootstrap"we were lucky with trade order" — shuffles your real trades 10k times
Walk-forward"it only worked in one period" — every 90-day window
Transaction-cost stress"fees don't matter"
Synthetic-data control"the machine is real, not lucky with structure"
Fill realism (intrabar)"the best price happened first" — 5m path inside each bar

Terms: permutation = random signals; bootstrap = reshuffle your own trades; walk-forward = out-of-sample window-by-window; null model = "what does pure luck produce?". Full beginner story: BEGINNER_GUIDE.md.
